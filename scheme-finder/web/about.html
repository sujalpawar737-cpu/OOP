About this system and its method

What it is

An Object Oriented Programming course project written in C++. A C++ program works as the web server: it serves these pages and answers their requests. The eligibility checking, benefit calculation and combination analysis all run in C++ classes.

How results are decided

  • Eligible based on the information provided: every criterion the system knows about is met by your answers.
  • Potentially eligible; verify with the official authority: no criterion fails, but an answer is missing or “Not sure”, or the scheme depends on competitive selection or an official beneficiary list.
  • Not eligible based on the information provided: at least one criterion is not met. The reason is shown.

Benefit estimates

Amounts are estimates from published scheme norms and from the numbers you enter (loan size, project cost, land area). The basis of every calculation is displayed. Insurance cover and future pension are shown separately and never added to cash. Where a figure needed an assumption (for example the rupee value of free grain), the assumption is stated.

Combination rules

Each restriction records its reason, a source link and a confidence label. official means it was found in the published guidelines of the scheme. conservative means the prototype is being cautious and the point should be confirmed. If no restriction is recorded between two schemes the system says so, but that is not a guarantee that they can be combined.

Recommendation

The program tries every conflict-free combination of the schemes open to you and picks the one with the highest score for your preference (money, cover or balanced) over your planning period. If two combinations score the same, the one with more schemes is chosen.

Dataset

About 56 schemes in five domains, taken from official and government-published sources and reviewed in October 2026. Schemes change often, so every scheme shows a “dataset checked” date and its official link. The data lives in plain text files in the data/ folder.
